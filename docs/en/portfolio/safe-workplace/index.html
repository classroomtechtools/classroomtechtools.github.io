Psychological Safety

💡When you hear the term “𝗣𝘀𝘆𝗰𝗵𝗼𝗹𝗼𝗴𝗶𝗰𝗮𝗹 𝗦𝗮𝗳𝗲𝘁𝘆”, what comes to mind?

I recently facilitated a session with Prasarana’s C-suite leaders. Parasarana is a Malaysia’s government-owned public transportation company.

In this session, we explored how one of their cultural beliefs, “𝘚𝘱𝘦𝘢𝘬 𝘜𝘱 𝘢𝘯𝘥 𝘓𝘪𝘴𝘵𝘦𝘯,” connects to building psychological safety at work.

We unpacked different perspectives and discussed what psychological safety looks like in practice. Also, we explored how leaders can strengthen their “𝘚𝘱𝘦𝘢𝘬 𝘜𝘱 𝘢𝘯𝘥 𝘓𝘪𝘴𝘵𝘦𝘯,” culture to create an environment where people feel safe to contribute with candour and confidence.

Moreover, we reflected on different aspect, such as:

  • What happens when you don’t speak up? What is the cost of silence?
  • How does feedback show up in their teams?
  • What is their underlying feedback culture feels like?
  • The risks of “Doing nothing”.
  • Things they want to “Continue doing”, “Start doing”, “Stop doing”.

Their insights on strengthening feedback and fostering a “Speak Up and Listen” culture were inspiring. They served as powerful reminders that psychological safety is “𝘵𝘩𝘦 𝘣𝘦𝘭𝘪𝘦𝘧 𝘵𝘩𝘢𝘵 𝘰𝘯𝘦 𝘤𝘢𝘯 𝘴𝘱𝘦𝘢𝘬 𝘶𝘱 𝘸𝘪𝘵𝘩𝘰𝘶𝘵 𝘧𝘦𝘢𝘳 𝘰𝘧 𝘱𝘶𝘯𝘪𝘴𝘩𝘮𝘦𝘯𝘵 𝘰𝘳 𝘩𝘶𝘮𝘪𝘭𝘪𝘢𝘵𝘪𝘰𝘯.” (𝘈𝘮𝘺 𝘌𝘥𝘮𝘰𝘯𝘥𝘴𝘰𝘯)

Most importantly, psychological safety is NOT about avoiding conflict. It is about creating space for candour where honest conversations drive growth, learning, and the pursuit of greater goals.
